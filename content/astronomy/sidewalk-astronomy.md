Sidewalk astronomy is about as simple as astronomy gets. You put a telescope on a busy sidewalk, point it at the Moon or a planet, and let anyone walking by have a look. It's free, and nobody has to plan anything.

I have been doing it in Tucson since 2022, mostly on University Boulevard next to the University of Arizona, and at public star parties around town.

{{< zl-photos >}}

## Why it matters

- **A lot of people have never looked through a telescope.** Many of the people who stop are seeing Saturn's rings or the Moon's craters with their own eye for the first time.
- **It reaches people an astronomy event never will.** Star parties and observatory nights draw people who already care. A sidewalk gets whoever is walking to dinner.
- **You don't need a dark sky for it.** The Moon and planets look the same from a lit street as they do from the desert.
- **It's the best case for dark skies.** Someone who has just seen Saturn is a lot easier to talk to about why the night sky is worth protecting.

None of this is my idea. John Dobson and the San Francisco Sidewalk Astronomers started doing it in the 1960s with big, cheap telescopes that anyone could build. The Dobsonian telescope is named after him.

I was doing public outreach long before the sidewalk. I helped with a lot of it at the Westport Astronomical Society's observatory in Connecticut, and in Arizona with Reach for the Stars Arizona, which brought telescopes to schools, libraries and scout troops. I still do a lot of outreach with the Huachuca Astronomy Club in Sierra Vista.

## What I bring

My big telescopes stay home for this. The targets are bright, and a telescope a kid can reach beats a bigger one on a ladder.

- **[8" f/6 Dobsonian](/astronomy/telescopes/).** Built by John Dobson himself around 1985, which makes it about the most fitting sidewalk telescope there is. Simple and sturdy, and it shows plenty. With a step stool, kids can use it on their own.
- **[6" f/4.3 Newtonian](/projects/6inch-f4/).** My first homemade telescope, with a mirror I ground in 2017.
- **[SVBONY MK127](/astronomy/telescopes/).** A small Maksutov that is very sharp on the Moon and planets.

## What you can see from a sidewalk

- The Moon's craters and mountains, best around first quarter.
- Saturn's rings.
- Jupiter's cloud bands and its four big moons.
- The phases of Venus.
- Bright double stars like Albireo.
