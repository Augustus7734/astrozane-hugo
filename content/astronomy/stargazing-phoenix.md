Phoenix is a big, bright city, and you cannot see the Milky Way from the middle of it. The good news is that an hour's drive fixes that. These are the places I take groups for [star parties in Phoenix and Scottsdale](/astronomy/starparties/phoenix/), from closest to darkest.

## The short list

| Spot | Drive | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Your own backyard or resort | None | The Moon, planets and bright star clusters |
| Fountain Hills | Just east of Scottsdale | A darker sky without leaving the Valley |
| Badger Springs Trailhead | About 50 minutes north, off I-17 | A quick trip up the freeway |
| Picketpost Trailhead | About an hour east, near Superior | A first real dark sky |
| Tonto National Forest | North and east of the city | The nearest big stretch of dark sky |
| Hovatter Road | About 90 minutes west of downtown | A properly dark, wide-open sky |
| Roosevelt Lake | About two hours east | A dark sky over the water, next to a certified dark sky park |
| Alamo Lake State Park | Northwest, an overnight trip | One of the darkest skies in Arizona |

Drive times are rough and depend on where in the Valley you start.

## Do you need to call ahead?

Mostly no. Trailheads, national forest land and other ordinary public land need no reservation and no phone call. You just go. National parks and state parks are the exception: they have gates, hours and their own rules, so call or check ahead. On this list that means Alamo Lake State Park, and Tonto National Monument at Roosevelt Lake.

## In the Valley

### Your backyard or resort

The Moon, Saturn, Jupiter, double stars and bright clusters look just as good from Scottsdale as they do from the desert. City light only hurts the faint things. If those are what you want to see, there is no need to drive anywhere.

### Fountain Hills

Just east of Scottsdale, and a certified International Dark Sky Community since 2018. The McDowell Mountains block some of the glow from the rest of the Valley. It is not a dark site, but it is the darkest sky you will find without getting on a highway.

## Worth the drive

### Badger Springs Trailhead

In Agua Fria National Monument, about 40 miles north of central Phoenix. Take I-17 to exit 256 and follow the dirt road for a little under a mile to the parking area. It is the easiest dark-sky drive from the north side of the Valley.

### Picketpost Trailhead

Near Superior, about an hour east of the Valley, with Picketpost Mountain on the horizon. It is close enough to reach after dinner and dark enough for the Milky Way. Both of the guests quoted below had their star parties here.

### Tonto National Forest

The nearest big stretch of dark sky to the Phoenix metro, north and east of the city. There are many pullouts and trailheads, and the farther you get from the Valley, the better the sky.

### Hovatter Road

An observing field off I-10 west of Tonopah, roughly 90 minutes from downtown Phoenix. It has been a favorite of Phoenix astronomy clubs for years, for good reason: flat ground and open horizons.

### Roosevelt Lake

In the Tonto National Forest, about two hours east of Phoenix. It is far enough from the Valley for a properly dark sky, and there are campgrounds along the shore if you want to stay. Tonto National Monument, on the south side of the lake, has been a certified International Dark Sky Park since 2019. The monument itself is open during the day only.

### Alamo Lake State Park

Remote, northwest of Phoenix, and one of the darkest places in Arizona you can reach on a paved road. It is too far for an evening trip, so plan to camp.

## Getting the most out of a night

- **Check the Moon first.** A bright Moon washes out the Milky Way and faint objects. For dark-sky viewing, go within about a week of new Moon.
- **Give your eyes time.** It takes 20 to 30 minutes to adapt to the dark, and one look at a white phone screen undoes it. Use a red light.
- **Know the season.** The bright summer Milky Way is in the evening sky from about June through October. Winter has Orion, the Pleiades and the brightest stars of the year.
- **Skip the worst of summer.** From late June through early September it is not fully dark until after 9 pm, it is still hot at midnight, and monsoon storms cancel a lot of nights.
- **Be ready for the desert.** Closed-toe shoes, water, a full tank, and a look at the map before you lose cell coverage.
