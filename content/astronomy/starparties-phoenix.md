## Made for Small Groups

Most of my star parties are for a handful of people, not a crowd. With a small group nobody waits long for the eyepiece, everyone gets their questions answered, and I can chase whatever your group gets excited about. Pricing is by the group, not per person, and covers up to 20 guests.

- **Families.** Screen-free, hands-on, and paced for whoever's there, from six-year-olds to grandparents. Viewing is seated, and winter sessions can start early enough to finish before bedtime.
- **Bachelor and bachelorette parties.** A first-night or wind-down activity for a Scottsdale or Phoenix weekend that the whole group can do together. I come to your rental or meet you at a dark-sky site, and can send you home with a group portrait under the stars.
- **Birthdays.** For kids or adults, in the backyard or out of town. Saturn's rings make a good surprise.
- **Couples.** Date nights, anniversaries, and proposals. A private telescope under a dark sky sets the scene; I'm happy to help plan the moment, photograph it, and otherwise stay out of the way.
- **Visitors and vacation rentals.** In Phoenix for a week, or have family in town? Seeing the desert sky properly is the thing they'll talk about when they get home.
- **Scout troops, clubs, and classes.** Great for badges and school groups; ask about longer sessions for bigger groups.

Planning something larger — a wedding, a corporate event, a resort program? I do those too. [Get in touch](https://astrozane.com/links/contact) and we'll shape it around your venue.

I build my own telescopes from scratch, grinding the mirrors by hand; I've tested and reviewed hundreds more; and I serve on the board of DarkSky Southern Arizona.

- **An unplugged experience.** No computers, no screens — everything is found and tracked by hand at the eyepiece, the way stargazing is meant to be. While you wait for a turn, there are binoculars to explore the sky with.

Alongside it come a 6" telescope, binoculars for guests, and a PVS-14 night vision device that pulls nebulae out of the sky even when conditions aren't ideal. A hand-built 24" telescope joins the lineup in spring 2027.
- **Photos to take home.** On request I'll shoot a timelapse of your night or a portrait of your group under the stars. The camera runs in the background; nobody looks at a screen while we're out there.
- **Fun for all ages.** Whether your group is kids, adults, or a mix, the experience stays engaging and easy to enjoy — no background knowledge needed.
- **Optional presentation.** I can add a short talk to set the scene before you head to the telescopes.
- **Dependable skies.** Arizona has some of the clearest, darkest skies in the country, which means far fewer weather cancellations than most places.
- **Built around you.** I'll work with your venue, schedule, and preferences to shape the right event for your group.

## Where to Stargaze Near Phoenix

The easiest star party is the one at your place: a backyard, rental, or resort anywhere in Phoenix, Scottsdale, Tempe, Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert, Glendale, Peoria, Fountain Hills, or Cave Creek. The Moon and planets look great from the middle of the city. When you want a darker sky, these are the places I like to take groups. Tell me where you're staying and what you want to see, and I'll recommend one.

- **Picketpost Trailhead** — near Superior, about an hour east of the Valley, with Picketpost Mountain on the horizon.
- **Tonto National Forest** — the nearest big stretch of dark sky to the Phoenix metro, north and east of the city.
- **Hovatter Road observing field** — off I-10 west of Tonopah, roughly 90 minutes from downtown. A longtime favorite of Phoenix astronomy clubs for good reason.
- **Alamo Lake State Park** — remote, northwest of Phoenix, and one of the darkest places in Arizona you can reach on a paved road. Ideal for an overnight.
