**Tucson is one of the few cities in America where you can still see the Milky Way from the edge of town. Come see what else is up there.**

I bring hand-built telescopes, binoculars, and night vision to your backyard, vacation rental, resort, or a dark-sky site near Tucson, and show your group the craters and mountains of the Moon, Saturn's rings and Jupiter's cloud bands and moons when they are up, double stars in gold and blue, clusters of thousands of stars like the Pleiades and the great cluster in Hercules, glowing clouds of gas like the Orion Nebula, and galaxies like Andromeda, whose light left 2.5 million years ago. ## Made for Small Groups

Most of my star parties are for a handful of people, not a crowd. With a small group nobody waits long for the eyepiece, everyone gets their questions answered, and I can chase whatever your group gets excited about. Pricing is by the group, not per person, and covers up to 20 guests.

- **Families.** Screen-free, hands-on, and paced for whoever's there, from six-year-olds to grandparents. Viewing is seated, and winter sessions can start early enough to finish before bedtime.
- **Bachelor and bachelorette parties.** A first-night or wind-down activity for a Tucson weekend that the whole group can do together. I come to your rental or meet you at a dark-sky site, and can send you home with a group portrait under the stars.
- **Birthdays.** For kids or adults, in the backyard or out of town. Saturn's rings make a good surprise.
- **Couples.** Date nights, anniversaries, and proposals. A private telescope under a dark sky sets the scene; I'm happy to help plan the moment, photograph it, and otherwise stay out of the way.
- **Visitors and vacation rentals.** In Tucson for a week, or have family in town? Seeing the desert sky properly is the thing they'll talk about when they get home.
- **Scout troops, clubs, and classes.** Great for badges and school groups; ask about longer sessions for bigger groups.

## Night Vision: Nebulae in Real Time

I use a PVS-14 night vision device to enhance views of nebulae in real time. We'll take a look at these and many other objects using regular eyepieces, too, but night vision reveals details that are often washed out by light pollution or even invisible to the unaided eye altogether.

These clips were filmed with a phone held up to the night vision eyepiece on my 14.7" telescope. Nothing is stacked, stretched, or processed afterward — this is the live view, as it happens, and a phone camera only catches part of what your eye picks up at the eyepiece.

## Where to Stargaze Near Tucson

The easiest star party is the one at your place: a backyard, rental, resort, or venue anywhere in Tucson, Oro Valley, Marana, the Catalina Foothills, Vail, Green Valley, Sahuarita, or Sierra Vista. When you want a darker sky, these are the places I like to take groups. Tell me where you're staying and what you want to see, and I'll recommend one. My full guide to [stargazing spots near Tucson](/astronomy/stargazing-near-tucson/) has drive times and tips for each.

- **Gabe Zimmerman Trailhead** — on the Arizona Trail near Vail, southeast of town. A quick drive, easy parking, and a real step up from city skies.
- **Saguaro National Park** — on the edge of the city on both sides, and a certified Urban Night Sky Place. Stargazing among the saguaros without a long drive.
- **Ironwood Forest National Monument** — open desert northwest of Tucson, past Marana, with wide horizons and a dark western sky.
- **Oracle State Park** — a certified International Dark Sky Park in the foothills north of Tucson, about an hour from town.
- **Chiricahua National Park** — America's newest national park and a certified International Dark Sky Park, about two hours southeast near Willcox. Some of the darkest skies in Arizona, above a landscape of stone spires.
